Portugal Visa: Applicants Now Asked for Proof of Paid Flights (July 2026 Update)

📅 July 8, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ SlotBot Team

Here's something worth knowing before your Portugal visa appointment. Over the last few weeks, some applicants have reported that, at submission, they were asked to show proof that their flights were actually purchased — a payment confirmation or receipt — rather than just a flight reservation. If you're preparing a Portugal Schengen visa application right now, this is a heads-up worth taking seriously.

⚠️ Important context first

This is based on recent applicant reports, not a confirmed change to the official rules. The official Schengen requirement is still a flight reservation, not a paid ticket. Always check the official TLScontact / VFS page for Portugal before your appointment — and read the safe approach below so you don't risk losing money.

What the official rule still says

On paper, nothing has changed. Across all Schengen countries, the documented requirement is a flight reservation or itinerary — not a fully paid, non-refundable ticket. In fact, the European Commission's own Schengen guidance advises applicants NOT to buy non-refundable tickets before a visa decision is made, precisely because approval is never guaranteed and travellers shouldn't risk large sums up front.

So officially, a reservation should be enough. That's the baseline.

What some applicants are reporting in practice

Practice and paperwork don't always line up. Recently, several applicants submitting for Portugal have said they were asked at the counter to show a transaction or receipt proving the flights were paid for — not just a dummy reservation. Whether this is a specific centre's practice, a particular officer's request, or the start of a wider shift isn't yet clear from official sources.

The key point: even if the official checklist says "reservation", the person in front of you at submission may ask for more. Being prepared for that avoids a nasty surprise on the day.

💡 Why this matters

If you turn up with only a basic reservation and you're asked for proof of payment, you could be sent away to sort it out — losing your hard-won appointment slot in the process. In a system where slots are scarce, that's a costly setback.

The safe way to handle it: refundable tickets

Here's how to cover both bases without gambling your money. Instead of a basic reservation or an expensive non-refundable ticket, use a fully refundable or flexible ticket. This gives you:

  • A real payment record — so if you're asked for proof of purchase, you have it.
  • Your money protected — if the visa is refused or delayed, you cancel and get refunded.
  • A verifiable booking reference (PNR) — which also satisfies the standard reservation requirement.

Refundable fares cost more than basic tickets, but far less than losing a non-refundable fare if your visa doesn't come through. For a high-stakes application, that's cheap insurance.

Before your appointment: a quick checklist

  1. Check the official source. Open the TLScontact / VFS page for Portugal and read the current document list.
  2. Prepare a refundable ticket or a verifiable reservation with a real PNR.
  3. Keep the payment confirmation handy — on your phone and printed — in case it's requested.
  4. Don't buy non-refundable flights before your visa is approved.
  5. Bring all standard documents: passport, application form, insurance (min €30,000), accommodation, proof of funds.

First things first: you still need the appointment

All of this only matters once you actually have an appointment — and for Portugal, as for the rest of Schengen, that remains the hardest part. Slots at the visa centre fill fast and cancellations vanish within minutes.
