Greece Visa from the UK: Why You Now Need Fully Paid Flight Tickets

📅 August 15, 2026⏱ 4 min read✍️ SlotBot Team

If you're applying for a Greek Schengen visa from the UK, there's one requirement that catches almost everyone off guard. Unlike nearly every other Schengen country, Greece requires proof that your round-trip flight tickets are fully paid — a receipt, invoice or payment confirmation — as part of your visa application. An unpaid reservation is not enough.

⚠️ The official rule

The requirements published by GVCW — the official visa application centre for Greece in the UK — state that applicants must present a receipt, invoice or payment confirmation demonstrating that round-trip flight tickets to and from Greece are fully paid. Source: GVCW official visa information page.

Why This Is Unusual

The standard advice for Schengen visas — repeated by consulates themselves — is the opposite: don't buy tickets before your visa is approved. Most Schengen consulates in the UK (France, Germany, Italy, Spain and others) accept an unpaid flight reservation or itinerary with a valid booking reference.

Greece flips this on its head. By demanding paid tickets, the Greek consulate shifts the financial risk onto you, the applicant: you must commit money to flights before knowing whether your visa will be approved — or approved in time.

The Risk You're Taking

  • The visa fee is not refunded if your application is refused
  • Processing takes 15 calendar days minimum — for some nationalities requiring consultation with other Schengen states, at least 3 weeks, and complex cases can take up to 45 days
  • Applying in Manchester or Edinburgh adds ~5 days, because applications are routed via the London centre to the Greek Embassy
  • If the decision comes after your departure date — or comes back negative — your ticket money is subject to the airline's fare rules

How to Protect Your Money

  1. Secure your appointment first, then buy tickets. All supporting documents must be less than 1 month old at the appointment anyway — so buying tickets right after your appointment is confirmed satisfies both rules and minimises the window of risk.
  2. Book flexible or refundable fares. Most airlines sell semi-flexible tickets that allow date changes or refunds minus a fee. The premium is usually far cheaper than losing the whole fare.
  3. Apply early. You can apply up to 6 months before travel and no later than 15 days before — but given current demand, GVCW itself warns processing may exceed the standard 15 days. Aim for 4–6+ weeks before your trip.
  4. Match your ticket dates to your application. Entry and exit dates on the tickets must be consistent with your itinerary, accommodation and insurance — mismatched dates are a classic refusal trigger.
  5. Don't rely on standard travel insurance. Most policies do not cover cancellation due to visa refusal — check the fare rules or a specific add-on if you want that protection.

Other Strict Greece Rules Worth Knowing

The paid-ticket rule is part of a generally stricter checklist for Greece. Also required:

  • All documents must be recent — not older than 1 month from the application date
  • Employment letter, not a contract — and it must also be less than 1 month old
  • Copies of previous Schengen visas, with recent or valid ones highlighted
  • Both parents must attend the appointment for a child's application (with limited exceptions)
  • Insurance must cover the whole Schengen area with minimum €30,000 coverage
  • Greece must be your main destination — the most days of your trip, or the first entry point if equal
