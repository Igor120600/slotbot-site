EU Proposes a Visa Ban for Russian Ex-Combatants and Stricter Tourist Visa Checks: What It Means for Your Schengen Application

📅 November 25, 2026⏱ 5 min read✍️ SlotBot Team

On 23 November 2026 the European Commission presented its 22nd package of sanctions against Russia. Headlines say the EU wants to "ban visas for Russians". The reality is narrower, and it matters if you are planning to apply for a Schengen visa.

⚠️ Short answer

This is not a ban on visas for all Russian citizens, and nothing has changed yet. The proposed ban targets Russians who serve or have served in the armed forces since the start of the invasion. It needs approval from all 27 EU member states before it takes effect. Separately, the Commission urged member states to take a very strict approach to tourist visas.

What the Commission proposed on 23 November

Two things were announced on the same day, and they are often mixed up:

Sanctions package (proposal)Updated visa guidelines (recommendations)
StatusProposal. Needs unanimous approval from the 27 member states.Recommendations to member states. Not automatically binding.
Who is affectedAnyone serving or who has served in the Russian armed forces since the invasion began, including foreign nationals who fought on Russia's side. Also athletes competing under the Russian flag.Russian applicants for tourist ("non-essential") visas in general.
What changesAn EU-wide visa ban for those groups.A "very strict approach" to tourist visas, tougher border checks, and non-recognition of five-year non-biometric passports.

What the updated guidelines recommend

  • Tourist visas: a very strict approach to non-essential travel, citing security risks.
  • Passports: member states should stop recognising Russian non-biometric (five-year) international passports. According to a Commission list reported by The Insider, 15 of the 29 Schengen countries already restrict them, including Czechia, Germany, France and Estonia.
  • Border checks: interviews, mandatory fingerprinting and individual assessment of Russian nationals entering the Schengen area.
  • Military service questions: applications could include questionnaires about military service and ask for military ID or service records.
  • Exceptions: the Commission says human rights defenders, opposition figures and independent journalists should not be affected, and it envisages exceptions for Ukrainians who were forced to take Russian citizenship.
💡 What is still unclear

Reporting notes that the proposal does not make clear whether it would apply to people called up for compulsory service since 2022 who did not take part in combat. Final wording can change during negotiations between member states.

What this means in practice

Nothing prevents you from applying today, and each Schengen country still decides on individual applications. But the direction of travel is clear: closer scrutiny, more questions and more document checks. Appointment slots at TLScontact, VFS and BLS in the UK are already hard to get, so if you plan to travel in the coming months it makes sense not to leave booking to the last minute.

Nobody can guarantee a visa decision, and booking an appointment earlier does not change the outcome of your application. It simply gives you more time and more options if rules or processing times change.

A practical checklist

  1. Check your passport. If you hold a five-year non-biometric passport, check whether the country you are applying to accepts it. Some already do not.
  2. Pick the right country and plan dates early. Each state sets its own processing approach.
  3. Prepare complete documents. Itinerary, accommodation, funds, insurance and purpose of travel, all consistent with each other.
  4. Be ready for extra questions at the visa centre or consulate, and answer them truthfully.
  5. Secure an appointment. Slots disappear within minutes, which is where monitoring helps.

Frequently asked questions

Has the EU banned Schengen visas for all Russians?

No. The proposal covers Russians who serve or have served in the armed forces since the invasion, plus athletes under the Russian flag. It is not a blanket ban.

Is the ban already in force?

No. It is part of a sanctions package that needs unanimous approval from the 27 member states.

Can Russians still apply for a Schengen visa?

Yes. Applications are still assessed by each Schengen country, but the Commission recommends a very strict approach to tourist visas.

Will my five-year passport be accepted?

It depends on the country. Check the requirements of the state you are applying to before you book an appointment.
